The dwell clock sets the laundry wage

A laundry app quotes twenty dollars a bag and leaves the denominator blank. The same bag runs on two clocks. The active clock holds about 59 minutes of real work: 24 minutes of driving, 35 of hauling, sorting, transfers, folding, and messages. The committed clock holds roughly 3 hours, because the wash and dry dwell chains you to the house even though the machines do the labor. One payout, two wages.

Take the standard bag. Twenty dollars minus seven dollars of mileage at the 70-cent IRS rate on a 10-mile loop, minus a dollar fifty in detergent and dryer power, nets $11.50. On the active clock that is about $11.70 an hour. On the committed clock it is about $3.80. The 90-to-150-minute dwell is semi-recoverable, overlappable with your own laundry or a remote job, so the honest rate lands somewhere between the two, positioned by how much of the cycle you genuinely reuse. The audit is mechanical: time one loop, split active minutes from dwell, log the round-trip miles, subtract miles times $0.70 and about $1.50 a load, then divide twice.

Route density flips which clock you live on. A suburban single order pays $11.70 active and $3.80 committed, near or below a high state's wage floor. The urban stacked route, three customers and four bags on a 12-mile loop, grosses $72, loses $8.40 to mileage and $6.00 to supplies, and nets $57.60: $28.80 an hour of active labor, $12.80 fully loaded. Same washer, same folding speed. Dispersed zones reward the multi-load order that amortizes one drive across three loads; dense zones reward stacking, where short legs multiply revenue per mile.

Taxes take a second cut. Gig laundry is 1099 income carrying the 15.3 percent self-employment tax plus income tax, and a 25 percent reserve turns the $28.80 active hour into $21.60 and the committed $12.80 into $9.60. The break-even runs backward: a $20-an-hour target on one active hour of a single suburban bag needs $20 plus $7.00 plus $1.50, a $28.50 offer that dispersed single bags rarely reach.

The payout table never quotes the denominator. ExtraHustles prices side income the way this bag was priced above, and the laundry hourly rate math runs the receipts-first ledger across Sudshare, Poplin, and Hampr, where the winner is picked by route density, not the app's payout table.
